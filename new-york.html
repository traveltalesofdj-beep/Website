New York, I’m Already Planning My Return

Five snowy days for Dillon’s birthday · by Dillon & Jovita

If you grew up watching Home Alone and every rom-com framed by a snowy Manhattan skyline, you’ll understand why New York felt less like a destination and more like a place I had been waiting my whole life to visit.

We went for Dillon’s birthday on 29 January, right in peak winter. The city was snow-covered, beautiful and very slushy — layers and waterproof shoes won over cute outfits. Even so, I left wanting to experience New York in every season.

Day one

Landing, lunch and a very expensive lesson

We landed around 11:30am, sailed through immigration and reached Arlo NoMad by about 2pm. Pre-booking a discounted cab from JFK made the tired, luggage-heavy arrival easy. The AirTrain and subway or LIRR can be cheaper, but allow roughly 60–75 minutes door to door.

After our first Chick-fil-A, we headed to Peak on the 101st floor of 30 Hudson Yards for Dillon’s pre-birthday drinks. The sunset was unreal, the team brought him a little cake, and then we discovered our Peak reservation included access to The Edge one floor below.

The $30 lesson

We had already bought separate Edge tickets. If you book Peak, ask about complimentary Edge access before paying for the observation deck too.

We ended the day around Hudson Yards and The Vessel, ate excellent tacos at Los Tacos No. 1, then braved the cold for night views and Dillon’s birthday reel on Brooklyn Bridge.

Day two

Oculus, a helicopter ride and sunset at The Edge

Apollo Bagels started the morning, followed by the Oculus, the 9/11 Memorial and Wall Street’s Charging Bull. Then we crossed to New Jersey for Dillon’s birthday surprise: a FlyNYON helicopter ride over Manhattan.

The first slot was 9am rather than true sunrise, but seeing the full skyline from above was still one of the best experiences of the trip. We squeezed in a huge Target shop before returning to Manhattan for the Edge sunset slot we had already paid for.

The night carried on through Grand Central, Rockefeller Center, another round at Los Tacos and Sephora, before Adel’s halal cart. Even in deep winter the food-cart queue took around 30 minutes — and it was completely worth it.

Day three

Snowy Central Park, DUMBO and a film-location pilgrimage

A full American breakfast set us up for Central Park under snow. As a huge Shah Rukh Khan fan, I had to find the Kal Ho Naa Ho locations, including Bow Bridge, and enthusiastically attempted a few recreations.

After The Met and lunch we headed to Brooklyn: the NYPD 78th Precinct exterior used in Brooklyn Nine-Nine, the classic DUMBO bridge viewpoint and another Brooklyn Bridge crossing for sunset. The bridge felt completely different in daylight.

Day four

Bagels, Cronuts, Friends and a 4am Times Square

Leon's Bagels and Dominique Ansel’s famous Cronut began a day built from food and TV memories. Then came The Friends Experience on East 23rd Street: recreated sets, the fountain, the pivot couch and Central Perk — everything a fan could want.

Magnolia Bakery’s banana pudding, Chinatown dumplings and a $3 roast-pork pineapple bun followed. L’Industrie in the West Village served what was, honestly, the best pizza slice I had eaten to date.

After a nap we got up around 4am and walked to an almost empty Times Square. No crowds, only the two of us and all those lights. It was one of the most surreal moments of the trip, finished with a Dunkin’ coffee on the walk back.

Day five

One last bagel and a reluctant goodbye

Our final morning meant one more Apollo Bagels run, Central Park near the Plaza, Fifth Avenue and a last set of photos. Joe’s Pizza was the fitting final meal before the airport.

I genuinely had tears in my eyes leaving. Spring, summer, autumn — I want to see this city in every version, and I cannot wait to return.

Know before you go

Practical tips for visiting NYC

  • Use the subway. Tap a contactless card or phone directly on the OMNY reader. It took us less than a day to get comfortable with the map.
  • Tipping is contextual. Around 15–20% is normal at sit-down restaurants; quick counter service and carts felt much more relaxed.
  • Book major museums ahead. Timed tickets can save long weekend queues at places such as MoMA and The Met.
  • Check sunrise times. A tour’s first helicopter slot may still be well after the actual winter sunrise.
  • Pack for slush. Layers and waterproof boots matter more than the outfit once snowy pavements turn muddy.
